HOA common areas.

Buckeye HOAs take care of miles of greenbelts and trails. Dog waste builds up fastest at trailheads, park edges, mailbox clusters and the turf near pools and clubhouses.

We scoop those spots on a set day, service every station and send the board a photo report. Many Buckeye parks already have stations. We keep them stocked with bags and emptied before they overflow.

Dog parks.

Verrado's Granite Bluff and Marketside dog parks are for residents, and HOA dog parks like them see dogs every day. We scoop the whole park, empty the cans, refill the bags and wipe down the stations.

Most dog parks need scooping once a week, and busy ones twice. In summer we come early in the morning, before the heat sets in.

Apartments and build to rent.

New apartment and build to rent communities keep opening in Buckeye, many near Interstate 10. Most allow dogs, and the pet relief areas fill up fast. We keep dog runs and relief areas clean and the stations stocked, so the grounds look good when a renter tours.

Businesses and events.

We also clean up for offices, stores, vet clinics, groomers and boarding in Buckeye. For dog friendly events, we bring loaner stations, stay on site and sweep the grounds after.

Buckeye rules.

Buckeye city code says animal waste cannot be left in a way that is not clean, on public land or private land. Off your own property, dogs must be on a leash. Most HOAs add their own pickup rules, and a clean common area makes them easy to follow.

A year of dog waste in Buckeye.

  • October to April: the best walking weather of the year. Trails and parks stay busy, and many 55 plus communities fill up with winter residents. Stations fill faster, so this is when many HOAs add a second weekly visit.
  • May and June: the heat arrives. People walk dogs at dawn and after dark, so waste piles up along the same few paths. We come early in the morning.
  • July to September: monsoon season. One storm can wash a week of waste into washes and drains, and wet waste smells worse in the heat. Keep the schedule tight.

Rock, turf and grass.

Most Buckeye common areas mix desert rock, artificial turf and some grass. On rock, dry waste blends in and gets missed. On turf, urine soaks into the backing and smells in the heat. On grass, waste kills the spot it sits on.

We walk each area in a pattern so nothing gets skipped. We can also add deodorizer spray on turf relief areas.
