No dog park in town.

Litchfield Park does not have its own dog park. That puts more dogs on sidewalks, greenbelts and HOA common areas, and those spots need regular pickup. A station by each walking path, with a full roll of bags, helps a lot.

Litchfield Park rules.

City code says dog waste must be picked up right away in parks, on public land and on any land you do not own. It goes in a closed bag and then in the trash. In city parks, dogs must be on a leash 6 feet long or shorter, and breaking the leash rule can bring a fine of up to $2,500.

Condos and small common areas.

Many Litchfield Park homes share small common areas. A few dogs on a small patch of grass add up fast, and smell hangs around in the heat. We scoop shared grass and rock areas, service the stations and send a photo report to your board or manager.

Resorts and golf communities.

Pet friendly rooms and golf course homes bring dogs onto lawns, paths and turf. We scoop those areas and keep stations stocked, early in the day before guests are out.

A year in Litchfield Park.

  • October to April: golf season and the best walking weather. Sidewalks and greenbelts stay busy all day.
  • May and June: walks move to dawn and after dark, and smell builds fast in the heat. We come early in the morning.
  • July to September: monsoon storms wash waste off lawns and into drains. Keep the schedule tight.

Shade trees and grass.

Litchfield Park has big old trees, golf course lawns and shady grass areas. Grass hides waste in the shade and under leaves, and waste kills the grass it sits on.

We walk grass areas in a pattern and check under trees and along hedges, where waste gets missed most.
