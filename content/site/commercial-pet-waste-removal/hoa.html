Hot spots

Where dog waste piles up in an HOA.

Waste builds up where walks start and where dogs stop. We check those spots first on every visit.

@@map@@

Trailheads and paths

Walks start here, and so do most piles. Long paths get a little at a time, all the way down.

@@park@@

Dog parks and park edges

Dogs every day in one small space, plus the grass just outside the gate.

@@station@@

Mailbox clusters

Residents walk the dog to get the mail. The grass around the boxes takes the hit.

@@sun@@

Pools and clubhouses

Turf by the pool and clubhouse gets used, and it smells fast in the heat.

@@hoa@@

Entrances and front greenbelts

The first thing visitors and buyers see when they drive in.

@@rain@@

Washes and retention basins

Out of sight, so waste sits. Then a monsoon storm carries it into the storm drains.

Schedule

How often should an HOA pick up dog waste?

Most HOA common areas need one visit a week. Dog parks and busy trailheads need two. From May to September, add a visit at your busiest spots, since heat makes smell and flies worse.

Common areaVisits a weekWhy
Greenbelts and trails1Steady traffic over a lot of ground.
Dog parks1 or 2Busy parks need 2.
Trailhead and dog park stations2A busy can fills up in 3 or 4 days.
Pools, clubhouses and mailbox clusters1Small spots, but residents notice them first.

We time every area on the free site walk and tell you what your community needs.

Stations

Where to put pet waste stations.

Put stations where walks start and where dogs stop. A station right next to the path gets used. One tucked behind a wall gets skipped.

  • At each entrance to the community
  • At every trailhead and along long paths
  • Next to each dog park and greenbelt
  • By mailbox clusters
  • Near pools, clubhouses and tot lots

Full bag rolls matter as much as the station. An empty dispenser teaches residents to skip it. See pet waste station service.
