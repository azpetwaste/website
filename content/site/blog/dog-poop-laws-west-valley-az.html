Each West Valley town has its own rule. Goodyear sets a number for yards: twice a week. Litchfield Park and Buckeye have rules for parks and public places. Citrus Park follows county rules, and most HOAs add their own.

We are not lawyers, and this is not legal advice. Codes change. Check with your city or your HOA for the rule that applies to you. We last checked these sources in November 2026.

Goodyear.

The City of Goodyear says pets are allowed as long as they do not become a nuisance from flies, bugs, smell or dust. The city also says manure or droppings must be removed from the property at least twice a week.

Code Compliance looks into a problem after a neighbor reports it. The owner first gets a notice and time to fix it. If the owner does not fix it, the city can write a civil citation.

Dogs in Goodyear must be on a leash no longer than 6 feet on public streets, in public parks and on public property.

Litchfield Park.

Chapter 6 of the Litchfield Park City Code has a section named Disposal of Fecal Matter. It says anyone with a dog in a park or on public property must pick up right away. The waste must go in a closed or sealed container and then in the trash.

A second section covers private property. If your dog goes on property you do not own, you must pick it up right away. The code treats that as a civil violation.

In a public park, a dog must be on a leash no longer than 6 feet, unless it is in a fenced dog area the city has set up.

Buckeye and Verrado.

Chapter 11 of the Buckeye city code covers parks. It says the owner of a pet must remove its solid waste from a city park or community services site. It also says no one may walk a dog without a leash, except in an area set up for it.

Chapter 6 says a dog may not run at large. A leash can be no longer than 6 feet.

We did not find a Buckeye rule that sets a number of pickups a week for your own yard. Verrado and most other Buckeye neighborhoods have an HOA, so check your HOA rules.

Citrus Park and Waddell.

Citrus Park and Waddell sit outside city limits, so Maricopa County rules apply. County Ordinance P 13 says dogs must be kept on the owner's property or on a leash no longer than 6 feet.

We did not find a county rule that sets how often you must scoop your own yard. In these areas, HOA rules are usually the ones that matter.

HOA rules and fines.

Most West Valley neighborhoods have an HOA. HOA rules often say you must pick up after your dog in common areas and keep your yard from causing smell.

Arizona's planned community law lets an HOA board set a reasonable fine for breaking the rules. First, the board must give you notice and a chance to be heard. Fine amounts are different in every HOA. Read your own rules to find yours.

If you get a letter, do three things:

  • Clean the yard the same day and take a photo.
  • Answer the letter in writing by the date on it.
  • Set up a pickup schedule so it does not happen twice.

The simple way to stay inside the rules.

Pick up at least once a week, and twice a week in Goodyear. Carry bags on every walk. Put the bag in a trash can with a lid.
