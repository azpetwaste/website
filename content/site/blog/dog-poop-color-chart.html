Healthy dog poop is chocolate brown, firm and easy to pick up. Most adult dogs go one to three times a day. A sudden change in color, shape or how often your dog goes is worth a closer look, and a few colors mean call the vet now.

Poop should look about the same from day to day if your dog eats the same food. One odd color after a new treat is usually nothing. A color that sticks around is worth a call.

What healthy dog poop looks like

  • Color: chocolate brown. Bile turns poop brown as it moves through the gut.
  • Shape: a log shape that holds together.
  • Feel: firm but not hard, like modeling clay. It should pick up without leaving much behind.
  • Coating: no slime, no grease and no blood.

Texture and other things to look for

  • Hard, dry pellets: your dog may need more water. Keep fresh water out all day, more so in summer.
  • Soft piles that will not hold a shape: often a food change, stress or a stomach bug. Call your vet if it lasts more than a day or two.
  • Watery diarrhea: can come from stress or parasites, and it can dry out a puppy fast.
  • Mucus or a slimy coating: a small amount can be normal. A lot of mucus, or mucus with blood, is a reason to call your vet.
  • Grass, string or bits of toys: your dog ate something it should not have. Watch for vomiting or straining, which can mean a blockage.

How many times a day should a dog poop

Most adult dogs go at least once a day, and two or three times a day is still normal. Puppies may go five times a day or more. Food, size and age all play a part, so learn what is normal for your dog.

Call your vet if your dog goes a full day without pooping, strains with nothing coming out, or suddenly goes far more often than usual.

When to call the vet right away

  • Black, tarry poop
  • A lot of blood, or poop that is all red
  • Any red in a puppy's poop
  • Diarrhea with vomiting or low energy
  • Diarrhea that lasts more than 48 hours
  • Bright green poop when your dog may have found rat or mouse poison

Keep an eye on it

Poop is one of the easiest ways to spot a health problem early. If you use a scooping service, still look at your dog's poop on walks or in the yard now and then, so you know what normal looks like.
